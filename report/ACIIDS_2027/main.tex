\begin{abstract}
Many group-robust methods on Waterbirds fit a light head on frozen features using a small validation set with group labels. We ask what limits such methods, taking diverse prototypical ensembles (DPE) as the case study: how the 1,199 validation images are spent, or what they are labelled. Three landbird species are labelled waterbird in the benchmark because its generator matches the keyword ``tern'' inside ``Eastern'' and ``Western''. This error is known, but its effect on validation-label methods has not been measured. On seven ResNet-50 backbones, these species account for 50--62\% of DPE's errors in its worst group, and refitting DPE after correcting only the 18 affected validation images raises worst-group accuracy on corrected test labels by 2.5\pp{} on every standard-ERM backbone (95\% interval $[1.8, 3.2]$), while the official labels score the same fix as a 0.8\pp{} loss. In contrast, spending the same validation set more carefully helps little: with validation-only selection over ten split seeds, cross-fitting with recalibration, learned combiners and threshold tuning do not beat DPE fitted on all images, and recalibration acts only as a decision-threshold shift. Correcting the labels also reverses the sign of a 0.5\pp{} method difference.
\keywords{Subpopulation shift \and Worst-group accuracy \and Label errors \and Prototype ensembles \and Waterbirds}
\end{abstract}
