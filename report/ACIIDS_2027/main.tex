\begin{abstract}
Strong group-robust methods on Waterbirds, such as deep feature reweighting and diverse prototypical ensembles (DPE), fit a light head on frozen features using the 1,199 group-annotated validation images. Three levers could improve such a method: aggregating its heads better, spending the validation images more efficiently, or cleaning their labels. We compare all three for DPE on the same seven ResNet-50 backbones under one protocol, with settings chosen on validation data only, strictly nested inner cross-validation, ten split seeds and paired per-backbone statistics. Weighting rules and learned combiners fitted on part of the validation set beat the smaller ensemble they are usually compared with, but not DPE fitted on all images; even the best of 840 fixed aggregation predictors, chosen on the test set, gains only 1.1\pp{}. Cross-fitting removes the cost of holding images back, but its recalibration reduces to a threshold shift, and with validation-selected settings it is 0.17\pp{} below DPE. Labels are the larger lever. Three landbird species are labelled waterbird because the dataset generator matches ``tern'' inside ``Eastern'' and ``Western''. Correcting only the 18 affected validation images raises DPE's worst-group accuracy on corrected test labels by 2.5\pp{} on every standard-ERM backbone (95\% interval $[1.8, 3.2]$), whereas the official labels score the same fix as a 0.8\pp{} loss and reverse the sign of a 0.5\pp{} method difference.
\keywords{Subpopulation shift \and Worst-group accuracy \and Ensemble aggregation \and Cross-fitting \and Label errors \and Waterbirds}
\end{abstract}
